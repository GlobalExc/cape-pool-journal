faqs:
- q: Why is there a brown stain where leaves sat on the pool floor?
  a: Tannins from leaves, especially from oak and other hardwood trees, leach into marbelite and leave tea-coloured marks. Shock, brush, and remove leaves more often.
- q: What causes blue-green stains in a pool?
  a: Usually copper. It comes from corroding heat-exchanger or pipe fittings when the pH runs low, or from some copper-based algaecides.
- q: Can I use household bleach or acid on pool stains?
  a: Use products made for pools and follow the label. Strong acid washes can damage the finish and should be done by a professional, often with the pool drained, which carries its own risks.
- q: How do I stop metal stains coming back?
  a: Keep the pH in range so fittings don't corrode, add a metal sequestrant regularly if your top-up water carries metals, and fix the source, such as a corroding fitting.

## Step 1: Identify the stain

| Colour | Likely cause | Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brown or tea-coloured patches under leaves | Tannins from leaves | Organic |
| Green or black spots that come back | Algae (black algae has roots) | Organic |
| Red-brown or rust | Iron from borehole water, rusted fittings or fertiliser | Metal |
| Blue-green or teal | Copper from fittings or algaecide | Metal |
| Purple, pink or black | Manganese | Metal |
| White crust at the waterline | Calcium scale | Mineral |

## Step 2: The spot test

With the pump off, use a pole or a sock on a pole:

1. **Chlorine test:** hold a little granular chlorine against the stain for 30 seconds. If it lightens, it's **organic**.
2. **Vitamin C test:** rub a crushed ascorbic-acid tablet on the stain. If it lightens, it's **metal**, most likely iron.

## Removing organic stains

1. Balance the pH to 7.2–7.6.
2. Shock the pool in the evening.
3. Brush the stained areas hard, every day for several days.
4. For stubborn spots, use a stain brush or a pool-safe enzyme product.

Black algae needs patience: brush hard, treat it directly, and keep the chlorine high for several days.

## Removing metal stains

1. Lower the chlorine and test the metals if you can.
2. Use a **pool stain remover** made for metals, as the label directs. These are often ascorbic-acid based.
3. Then add a **metal sequestrant** to keep the dissolved metals in solution so they don't re-stain.
4. Rebalance the water afterwards.

## Removing scale

Calcium scale at the waterline comes off with a tile cleaner made for pools and a scourer. To stop it coming back, keep the pH and calcium in range.

## Prevention

- Keep the pH steady. Low pH corrodes metal fittings.
- Empty leaves before they sit on the floor.
- Test borehole water before using it to top up the pool.
- Use a sequestrant if your top-up water carries metals.

## In Cape Town: two common culprits

- **Borehole top-ups:** many Cape Town homes top up from boreholes or well points. These can carry iron or manganese, which stain the pool when chlorine is added. Test the water first or use a sequestrant.
- **Autumn leaves:** oak and other leafy trees in older suburbs drop tannin-rich leaves. Skim daily in autumn.
