- q: When should I start getting my pool ready for summer in Cape Town?
  a: Start in late September or early October. The winter rain has mostly stopped, the water is warming up, and algae starts growing quickly once it's above about 20°C. Starting early means a small clean-up instead of a green-pool rescue in December.
- q: How long does it take to get a green pool summer-ready?
  a: A pool that is only dull or slightly cloudy usually clears in 2–3 days. A fully green pool can take 4–7 days of shocking, brushing, filtering and backwashing. Very neglected pools may need draining, which a professional should assess first.
- q: Should I drain my pool before summer?
  a: Usually not. Draining wastes water, which matters in Cape Town, and can damage the pool shell if the groundwater is high. Most pools can be brought back with chemicals and filtration. Drain only if the water is badly unbalanced or the surface needs repair.
- q: Do I need to add salt in spring if I have a salt chlorinator?
  a: Often yes. Heavy winter rain overflows the pool and dilutes the salt. Test the salt level and top up to the range your chlorinator's manual gives, usually around 3,000–4,000 ppm, before you switch the cell back to summer output.

## Why spring prep matters more in Cape Town

Cape Town pools have a hard winter in a different way to inland pools. Between May and August, frontal rain tops up and overflows the pool, diluting chemicals and salt. Wind fills it with leaves, pine needles and sand. Many owners also cut pump hours to save power. By September the water is usually unbalanced, low on sanitiser and ready to turn green as soon as it warms up.

An afternoon of work in early October saves you a week of fighting algae in December.

## Step 1: Clear the debris

Get the big stuff out before you touch any chemicals. Leaves and organic matter use up chlorine and stain marbelite.

- Skim the surface and empty the skimmer and pump baskets.
- Remove the winter cover if you use one. Clean it and dry it before storing it.
- Scoop leaves off the floor with a leaf net before running the cleaner. Thick debris will clog it.

## Step 2: Brush, vacuum and backwash

Brush the walls, steps and floor, working towards the main drain. Brushing loosens algae and biofilm that the cleaner misses. Then vacuum to waste, or run your automatic cleaner.

Backwash the sand filter until the sight glass runs clear, usually 2–3 minutes. Then rinse for about 30 seconds. If the filter sand is older than about five years and the pool never stays clear, it may need replacing (see our upcoming guide on filter sand).

## Step 3: Test and balance the water in the right order

Test with a reliable kit or bring a water sample to your pool shop. Fix things in this order, because each one affects the next:

| What to test | Target range | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total alkalinity | 80–120 ppm | Holds pH steady. Fix this first. |
| pH | 7.2–7.6 | Chlorine works poorly above 7.8. Low pH etches surfaces. |
| Free chlorine | 1–3 ppm | Kills bacteria and algae. |
| Stabiliser (CYA) | 30–50 ppm | Protects chlorine from the strong summer sun. |
| Calcium hardness | 200–400 ppm | Cape Town water is soft, so this is often low. |
| Salt (salt pools only) | Per your unit's manual (often 3,000–4,000 ppm) | Rain dilutes it over winter. |

Add one chemical at a time with the pump running. Wait a few hours between additions and retest.

## Step 4: Shock the pool

Once the pH is in range, shock the pool in the evening. Use a large dose of chlorine so the sun doesn't burn it off. This clears out winter contamination and any algae that's starting. Follow the dose on the product for your pool's volume. Don't swim until the free chlorine is back to 1–3 ppm, usually the next day.

## Step 5: Check your equipment

Spring is the time to find problems, before the hottest week of the year does it for you.

- **Pump:** Listen for grinding or screeching, look for leaks at the lid O-ring, and check the timer settings.
- **Automatic cleaner:** Check the hoses, diaphragm and footpad for wear.
- **Salt cell:** Look for white scale on the plates. Clean it if needed, following the manufacturer's instructions.
- **Lights and electrics:** Any tripping or exposed wiring needs a qualified electrician.
- **Safety:** Check that the pool fence, gate latch and safety net are in good condition before children are back in the garden.

## Step 6: Set your summer pump schedule

In summer, most residential pools need the full volume filtered at least once a day, usually 8–10 hours of pump time. Split it into two blocks if you work around load-shedding or a time-of-use tariff, and run during the hottest part of the day when you can.

## In Cape Town: three local things to watch

1. **The south-easter.** From November the wind brings sand and fine dust, and it speeds up evaporation. Expect to empty baskets more often and top up the water weekly.
2. **Soft municipal water.** Low calcium water slowly pulls calcium out of marbelite and grout. Keep calcium hardness in range.
3. **Water restrictions.** Check the City of Cape Town's current water rules before refilling or topping up, especially after a dry winter.
