faqs:
- q: Is it better to run the pool pump during the day or at night?
  a: During the day is usually better, especially in summer, because that's when sunlight and swimmers use up chlorine and algae grows fastest. If you're on a time-of-use tariff, split the hours between cheaper periods and the hottest part of the day.
- q: Can I split pump hours into two blocks?
  a: Yes. Two blocks, for example morning and afternoon, work well and are easier to fit around load-shedding or tariff periods. What counts is the total filtration time each day.
- q: Will running the pump 24 hours a day damage it?
  a: Pumps are built to run continuously, and 24-hour running is normal while clearing a green or cloudy pool. For everyday use it just wastes electricity.
- q: Do salt chlorinators need longer pump times?
  a: Often yes. A salt cell only makes chlorine while water flows through it. If the chlorine stays low, you may need more pump hours or a higher output setting.

## The rule of thumb

| Season | Pump hours per day | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Summer (Dec–Feb) | 8–10 | Heat, sun and swimmers use up chlorine fast |
| Spring and autumn | 6–8 | Moderate temperatures |
| Winter (May–Sept) | 4–6 | Algae grows slowly in cold water |
| Clearing a green or cloudy pool | 24 | Filtering out dead algae and particles |

## Work it out for your pool

You want at least one **turnover** a day in summer, where the full volume passes through the filter once.

**1. Find your pool volume** (for a rectangular pool):

Length (m) × width (m) × average depth (m) × 1,000 = litres

*Example: 8 m × 4 m × 1.4 m × 1,000 = 44,800 litres*

**2. Find your pump's flow rate** in litres per hour. It's on the pump label or in the manual. Real-world flow is usually lower than the label once you count pipes and the filter, so knock off about a quarter.

**3. Divide:**

*44,800 L ÷ 9,000 L/h ≈ 5 hours per turnover*

In summer, aim for 1.5 to 2 turnovers, so this pool needs about **8–10 hours**.

## When to run it longer

- Heatwaves (30 °C and above)
- After pool parties or heavy rain
- When trees drop leaves into the pool
- When the chlorine keeps testing low

## How to save electricity

- **Keep the filter clean.** A clogged filter makes the pump work harder for less flow.
- **Empty the baskets often.** Blocked baskets starve the pump.
- **Use a timer.** Set it once per season instead of running the pump "just in case".
- **Consider a variable-speed pump** when your old one needs replacing. Running slower for longer uses far less power.
- **Use a cover.** Less debris means less filtering.

## In Cape Town: power cuts and tariffs

Many homes here plan pump times around load-shedding schedules or time-of-use tariffs. Split your hours into two blocks so a power cut doesn't wipe out a whole day's filtering. Reset the timer after outages, because some mechanical timers lose their place.
