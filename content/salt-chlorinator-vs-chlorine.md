faqs:
- q: Is a salt pool chlorine-free?
  a: No. A salt chlorinator turns dissolved salt into chlorine. The difference is that you don't add chlorine by hand, and the salt level is far lower than seawater.
- q: How long does a salt cell last?
  a: It depends on the brand, how many hours it runs and how well it's looked after. Keeping the pH and calcium in range and cleaning off scale extends its life.
- q: Do salt pools damage paving and coping?
  a: Salty splash-out can affect some natural stone and metal fittings over time. Rinse the surrounds now and then, and choose salt-tolerant materials and sealers.
- q: Can I convert my chlorine pool to salt?
  a: Usually, yes. You add a chlorinator and cell to the plumbing after the filter, then add pool salt to the level the manufacturer recommends. Check that your fittings and surrounds suit salt first.

## How each system works

**Manual or floater chlorine:** you add granular, liquid or tablet chlorine directly, or let a floater dissolve tablets slowly.

**Salt chlorinator:** you dissolve pool salt in the water. As water flows through the cell, electrolysis turns the salt into chlorine. The chlorine sanitises the water and then reverts to salt, and the cycle repeats.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Salt chlorinator | Manual or floater chlorine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upfront cost | Higher (unit, cell and salt) | Low |
| Running cost | Salt top-ups, acid, electricity, cell replacement | Ongoing chlorine purchases |
| Dosing | Automatic while the pump runs | By hand, or a floater refilled often |
| Water feel | Many find it softer on eyes and skin | Can smell "chlorinated" if not kept balanced |
| pH | Tends to rise, so needs regular acid | Depends on the product. Tablets lower it. |
| Maintenance | Clean the cell and check the salt level | Store and handle chemicals safely |

## Who suits salt

- Households that want steady chlorine without daily dosing
- Families with children who swim often
- Owners who can test the pH weekly and add acid

## Who suits manual chlorine

- Lower budgets or holiday homes used occasionally
- Pools where you, or your service provider, already dose every week
- Older pools with fittings that may not suit salt

## In Cape Town: two things to watch with salt

1. **Winter rain dilutes the salt.** After a wet winter, test the salt level before summer and top it up. A low-salt warning is common in spring.
2. **Soft water and pH drift.** Salt cells push the pH up, and Cape Town's soft water has little buffering. Keep the alkalinity in range and check the pH weekly. See our [pool chemical levels chart](/pool-chemical-levels-chart/).

## Our view

Both systems work well when the water is balanced. The best choice is the one you, or your service, will maintain consistently.
