faqs:
- q: How often should I vacuum my pool?
  a: Once a week in summer is enough for most Cape Town pools with an automatic cleaner, and more often after storms, strong wind or a green-pool treatment. Pools without an automatic cleaner need vacuuming once or twice a week.
- q: Why does dirt blow back into the pool while I vacuum?
  a: Usually the filter is full, the multiport valve is on the wrong setting, or the sand has channelled. Backwash, check the valve is on Filter or Waste, and vacuum more slowly so you don't stir up fine dust.
- q: Should I vacuum to waste or to filter?
  a: Vacuum to waste for dead algae, flocculated dirt or very fine dust, because the filter can't hold it. Vacuum to filter for normal leaves and sand. Waste loses water, so top up afterwards and check water restrictions.
- q: Can a robotic or automatic cleaner replace manual vacuuming?
  a: Mostly, yes. An automatic cleaner handles day-to-day dirt. You'll still need a manual vacuum for corners and steps, and after a big clean-up like a green pool.

## What you need

- A telescopic pole
- A vacuum head (a weighted one with brushes works best on marbelite)
- A vacuum hose long enough to reach the far end of the pool
- A skimmer plate, or a dedicated vacuum point on the pool wall

## Step 1: Clear the big debris first

Net out leaves and empty the skimmer and pump baskets. A vacuum chokes on leaves, and a full basket cuts suction.

## Step 2: Choose filter or waste

Set the multiport valve **with the pump off**. Never turn the handle while the pump is running.

| Setting | Use it for | Water loss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Filter | Everyday sand, small leaves, light dust | None |
| Waste | Dead algae, flocculated dirt, very fine dust | Yes, top up afterwards |

## Step 3: Prime the hose

This is the step most people skip. Air in the hose makes the pump lose prime, and it can damage the pump.

1. Attach the head to the pole and the hose to the head.
2. Lower the head to the floor near the skimmer.
3. Feed the hose into the water bit by bit, or hold the open end over a return jet, until bubbles stop coming out of the head.
4. Keep the hose end under water and connect it to the skimmer plate or vacuum point.

## Step 4: Vacuum slowly

Start the pump. Work from the shallow end towards the deep end in long, overlapping strips, like mowing a lawn. Go slowly. If you move fast, fine dust lifts into the water and settles again an hour later.

Watch the pressure gauge. If it climbs about 10 kPa above its clean reading, stop, backwash and carry on.

## Step 5: Finish up

- Turn the pump off before you disconnect the hose.
- Backwash and rinse if you vacuumed to filter.
- Set the valve back to Filter and restart the pump.
- Top up the water if you vacuumed to waste, then retest the pH and chlorine.

## Common mistakes

- **Turning the valve with the pump running.** This damages the valve's spider gasket.
- **Not priming the hose.** You'll hear the pump gurgle and the suction will drop.
- **Vacuuming dead algae to filter.** Fine algae passes straight through sand and goes back into the pool.
- **Rushing.** Slow strokes clean better than quick ones.

## In Cape Town: wind-blown sand

On south-easter days, fine sand and dust settle across the floor overnight. If your automatic cleaner can't keep up, give the deep end a manual vacuum once the wind drops. Don't do it during the wind, because more dust will settle straight away.
