faqs:
- q: Can I service my pool every two weeks in summer?
  a: It can work for a lightly used pool with no trees nearby and an owner who tests and adds chlorine in between. Most Cape Town pools drift out of balance within a week in summer, especially after a heatwave or a south-easter.
- q: What should a professional pool service include?
  a: Skimming, emptying baskets, brushing, vacuuming, backwashing, testing pH, chlorine and alkalinity, dosing chemicals, and checking the pump, cleaner and salt cell. Ask whether chemicals are included in the price or charged separately.
- q: Is a pool service worth it compared to DIY?
  a: DIY is cheaper in cash if you're consistent. A service is worth it if you're often away, if your pool keeps turning green, or if mistakes are costing you in wasted chemicals and repairs.
- q: Do I still need a service in winter?
  a: Yes, but less often. Algae grows slowly in cold water, but leaves, storms and rain still unbalance the pool. Fortnightly visits and shorter pump hours are typical.

## The quick guide by season

| Season | Months | Suggested service | Pump run time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Summer | Dec–Feb | Weekly | 8–10 hours a day |
| Shoulder | Oct–Nov, Mar–Apr | Weekly | 6–8 hours a day |
| Winter | May–Sept | Every 1–2 weeks | 4–6 hours a day |

These are starting points. Your pool's size, shade, trees and how much it's used all shift the numbers.

## What a proper service should cover

A good weekly service is more than a quick chlorine top-up. Check that yours includes:

1. Skimming the surface, and emptying the skimmer and pump baskets
2. Brushing the walls, steps and waterline
3. Vacuuming, or checking the automatic cleaner is working
4. Backwashing and rinsing the filter when the pressure says so
5. Testing pH, free chlorine and alkalinity, with calcium and stabiliser checked monthly
6. Dosing chemicals and recording the readings
7. Checking the pump, timer, cleaner hoses and salt cell, and reporting problems early

## What you can do between visits

- Run the pump on schedule, and adjust the timer around load-shedding.
- Skim leaves after windy days.
- Top up the water when the level drops below the middle of the skimmer opening.
- After heavy rain or a pool party, add a little chlorine or message your service provider.

## Signs you need more frequent service

- The pool turns cloudy or green between visits
- You're using noticeably more chlorine than you used to
- Trees drop leaves or pine needles into the pool
- The pool is used every day by children or guests, or you rent the property out

## In Cape Town: the wind factor

The south-easter changes pool care here. On a windy week, a pool can lose several centimetres of water to evaporation and fill with sand and leaves. Chlorine and pH drift faster too. That's the main reason weekly summer service is the norm in Cape Town, even for small pools.
