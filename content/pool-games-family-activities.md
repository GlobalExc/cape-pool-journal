- q: What pool games work for toddlers?
  a: Keep toddlers on the steps or in arm's reach. Pouring and splashing games, floating rubber ducks, kicking practice holding the edge, and singing games like Ring-a-Ring-o'-Roses in shallow water all work well.
- q: What can adults do in the pool besides swim?
  a: Water aerobics, aqua jogging, pool volleyball, relay races and float-and-chat afternoons. Water supports your weight, so pool exercise is gentle on the joints.
- q: How do I keep a pool party safe?
  a: Name a 'water watcher' who stays sober and off their phone, and swap every 15–20 minutes. Keep weaker swimmers in the shallow end, have no running on the paving, and keep the gate closed when the pool isn't in use.
- q: How do I keep the pool clean during a busy weekend?
  a: Shower before swimming, keep the pump running longer, check the chlorine morning and evening, and shock the pool after the event.

## Before you start: the one rule

**One adult is always the water watcher.** No phone, no book, no braai duty. Swap every 15–20 minutes. Drowning is quick and silent, and it happens even when adults are nearby.

## For toddlers (with an adult in arm's reach)

1. **Duck rescue:** Float rubber ducks and let toddlers "rescue" them from the steps.
2. **Pour and splash:** Cups, watering cans and sieves on the top step.
3. **Kick-kick:** Hold the edge and kick. Count kicks together.

## For kids

4. **Marco Polo:** The classic. "It" closes their eyes and calls "Marco"; everyone else answers "Polo."
5. **Sharks and minnows:** One shark in the middle, minnows try to cross without being tagged.
6. **Ring dive:** Throw coloured dive rings or sticks into the shallow end and collect them by colour.
7. **Treasure hunt:** Hide weighted toys and give clues. Works well for birthday parties.
8. **Red light, green light:** Walking or swimming version, in the shallow end.
9. **Noodle races:** Ride a pool noodle "horse" across the width.

## For teens and adults

10. **Pool volleyball:** Use a rope or a pool net across the middle and a beach ball.
11. **Relay races:** Mixed teams, any stroke, or the hold-a-ball-above-your-head version.
12. **Chicken fight:** Only in waist-deep water, with no dunking, and with matched sizes.
13. **Water aerobics:** Put a playlist on and do jogging, jumping jacks and arm circles. It's easy on the joints.

## For relaxed afternoons

14. **Float-and-read:** Inflatable loungers, a shaded side of the pool and plenty of sunscreen.
15. **Evening glow swim:** Waterproof LED lights or glow sticks in clear bags for a summer evening.

## Keep the water clean for the next day

A busy pool day uses up a lot of chlorine. Sunscreen, sweat and bodies all add up.

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Before | Test and top up chlorine. Ask everyone to rinse off. |
| During | Run the pump all day, and empty the baskets at lunch. |
| After | Shock in the evening, brush, and backwash the next morning. |

## In Cape Town: sun and wind

UV in Cape Town summers is high. Use SPF 30+ and rash vests, and play in the shade between 11:00 and 15:00. On south-easter days, pick sheltered games and keep inflatables tied down or out of the pool. The wind can blow them, and children, into the deep end.
