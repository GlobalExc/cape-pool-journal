- q: Is a pool net or a pool fence better?
  a: A fence is the stronger first layer because it works without anyone remembering to put it back. A net or rigid cover adds a second layer. Many families use both.

  a: South Africa's national standard for pool safety barriers, SANS 10134, is the reference most installers and municipalities use. - q: At what age can children swim without supervision?
  a: There's no safe age for children to swim with no adult watching. Even strong young swimmers can get into trouble. An adult should always be actively watching.

## Why layers matter

Most child drownings at home happen in a few quiet minutes when everyone thought someone else was watching. That's why safety experts talk about **layers**: if one fails, the next one catches it.

1. **Barriers:** a fence, gate, net or cover
2. **Supervision:** an adult watching the water and nothing else
3. **Skills:** swimming lessons and water-safety rules
4. **Response:** knowing CPR and having a phone nearby

## Layer 1: Fences and gates

A good pool fence:

- Fully separates the pool from the house and the rest of the garden
- Is high enough and has no footholds a child could climb
- Has gaps too narrow for a small child to squeeze through
- Has a **self-closing, self-latching gate** that opens outwards, away from the pool, with the latch out of a child's reach

**Check the standard.** In South Africa, SANS 10134 sets the requirements for pool safety barriers, and local building rules may refer to it. A reputable installer will know the current details.

**Keep climbing aids away.** Chairs, pot plants, braai stands and toys left near the fence give children a way over.

## Layer 2: Nets and covers

| Option | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Safety net | Affordable, lets the pool keep filtering, can take a child's weight if properly tensioned | Must be refitted after every swim, and anchors wear |
| Rigid or slatted safety cover | Strong, also cuts evaporation and debris | Expensive |
| Solar or bubble cover | Warms the water, cuts evaporation | **Not a safety device.** A child can slip under it. |

Check the net's tension and anchors at the start of every summer.

## Layer 3: Supervision rules

- **A named water watcher.** One adult with no phone, no book and no braai. Swap every 15–20 minutes.
- **Arm's reach for toddlers and weak swimmers.**
- **No swimming alone,** at any age.
- **Clear the pool area after swimming.** Floating toys tempt children back.

## Layer 4: Skills and response

- Enrol children in swimming lessons from an early age.
- Teach them to always ask before going near the water.
- Learn child and adult CPR, and refresh it every couple of years.
- Keep a charged phone and emergency numbers by the pool.

## Check your pool before summer

- [ ] Gate closes and latches by itself from any open position
- [ ] No gaps, broken panels or climbable objects along the fence
- [ ] Net anchors firm and the net tight
- [ ] Pool water clear enough to see the main drain
- [ ] Pump and drain covers intact (a broken suction cover can trap hair or limbs)

Clear water is a safety issue too. If you can't see the floor, you can't see someone in trouble.
