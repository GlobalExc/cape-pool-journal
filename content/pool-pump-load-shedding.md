- q: Can power cuts damage my pool pump?
  a: Surges and dips when the power returns can damage pump motors and electronic timers. A surge protector at the pool distribution board, fitted by a qualified electrician, is cheap insurance.
- q: Can I run my pool pump on an inverter or solar?
  a: Yes, if the system is sized for it. Pumps draw a large start-up current. A variable-speed pump uses far less power and is much easier to run on solar or battery. Get an installer to check before you connect it.
- q: Do I need to do anything after the power comes back?
  a: Check that the pump restarted, the timer still shows the right time, and water is flowing with no air in the pump basket. If the pump runs dry, switch it off and re-prime it.

## Plan around the schedule

Your pool needs a total amount of filtering each day (see [how long to run your pool pump](/how-long-to-run-pool-pump/)). When the power cuts out, that time doesn't have to be in one block.

1. Check your area's outage schedule each week.
2. Split pump time into **two or three blocks** that fall between outages.
3. Put the longest block in the late morning to afternoon, when the sun and swimmers use the most chlorine.

## Timers after outages

- **Mechanical dial timers** stop during an outage and then run late by however long the cut lasted. Reset them often.
- **Digital timers** usually keep time with a backup battery. Check it's working.
- **Smart plugs or Wi-Fi timers** are convenient, but make sure they're rated for the pump's load and installed by an electrician.

## Protect the pump

- Install **surge protection** for the pool circuit.
- Make sure the pump has an **earth leakage** device and that the wiring is in good condition.
- If the pump hums but doesn't start after a cut, switch it off. The capacitor may have failed, and leaving it on can burn out the motor.

## Restart safely

After the power comes back:

1. Check water is flowing and the pump basket is full of water, not air.
2. If the pump is running dry, switch it off, fill the basket with water, close the lid tightly and restart.
3. Check the pressure gauge is in its normal range.

## On heavy outage days

- Keep the free chlorine at the upper end of the range (around 3 ppm).
- Brush the walls and floor so algae can't settle.
- Skim by hand to keep debris out of the water.

## Is solar worth it for a pool pump?

A pool pump is one of the biggest electricity users in many homes. If you're installing solar anyway, a **variable-speed pump** makes the most of it: it runs slowly for long periods during sunny hours with a small, steady draw. Get a qualified installer to size the system.
